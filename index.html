About

I'm a PhD student in Chemical Engineering with a concentration in Materials Science & Engineering at the University of Notre Dame.

My research focuses on the structural and functional characterization of thermoelectric polymers and metal-organic supramolecular cage (MOSC)/polymer systems — materials with the potential to convert heat directly into electricity.

I am currently preparing for synchrotron beamtime at Argonne National Laboratory (12-ID-B/D) to study solvent vapor-induced phase separation (SVIPS) in MOSC/polymer composites using time-resolved SAXS/WAXS.

Research

Active Project

MOSC/Polymer Phase Separation via SVIPS

Investigating solvent vapor-induced phase separation in metal-organic supramolecular cage and polymer composite systems using time-resolved synchrotron SAXS/WAXS at Argonne National Laboratory (12-ID-B/D). Capillary geometry with ambient humidity as the SVIPS trigger, with blade-coated films as a secondary approach.

Thermoelectrics

Thermoelectric Polymer Systems

Structural and electronic characterization of conjugated polymers engineered for thermoelectric applications. Research emphasizes correlating microstructure to charge transport properties using WAXS, EIS, and CV measurements.

Characterization

Advanced Materials Characterization

Broad expertise in characterizing functional polymer systems using FTIR, SAXS/WAXS, XRD, SEM, DSC, TGA, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), and cyclic voltammetry (CV).
